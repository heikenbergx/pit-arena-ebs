Play from the Twitch panel — no typing

Everything on this page can be done in chat, but the Twitch extension panel is the easier way. It shows your own fighter — your gear, your stash, your gold — and you tap instead of typing. Nothing you do in the panel shows up in chat, so you can browse your bag mid-fight without spamming the stream.

Where to find it

  • Desktop: just under the video player, below the stream title. Click the panel and your fighter loads.
  • Phone / tablet: open the channel in the Twitch app, then the Extensions tab under the stream.
  • First time only, Twitch asks you to grant access so it knows which fighter is yours. One tap, then it remembers you.

What you can do there

  • Queue any fight — hunt, veteran, boss, nightmare, or the raid boss.
  • Compare before you equip. Hold or hover a stash piece and it shows exactly what changes, including which armour set bonuses it would switch on or drag down.
  • Train, ascend, and buy from the arena shop without looking up a command.
  • See your real stats — what's capped, what's still gaining, and why.
  • Rearrange it. The ▲▼ arrows on each section move it up or down, and it stays that way for you.

Chat commands still work exactly as before, and always will — the list below is the full reference. Use whichever you prefer; they're the same game.

STEP 1
!checkin

Creates your fighter and pays you gold. Do it once per stream — every hundredth check-in is a guaranteed rare or better.

STEP 2

STEP 3

How it all works

The short version of every system, so you know what you're spending gold on.

Levels & XP

Every fight pays XP, win or lose. The curve gets steeper as you climb, and the cap is level 50.

Higher-level hunters earn more XP from tough monsters and less from easy ones, so farming fodder forever stops paying.

!train converts gold straight into XP when you'd rather buy progress than fight for it.

The five fight tiers

Fodderany level · the reliable win
Veteranlevel 5 · tougher, better loot
Lieutenantlevel 10 · a genuine test
Bosslevel 20 · best odds at a unique
Nightmarelevel 30 · strips your best stats

Monsters scale to you, so nothing ever becomes filler — but the higher tiers stay genuinely dangerous.

Gear rarity

Common Magic Rare Unique Set Relic

Seven slots: weapon, helm, armour, gloves, boots, ring, amulet. Items are sidegrades with tradeoffs, not a straight ladder — a big damage weapon may cost you armour.

Each item also has an item level (iLv). Drops are banded to your level, so you get gear you can actually use.

Lesser · standard · Greater

Many items exist in three power versions. Lesser pieces drop for newer fighters, Greater ones for veterans, with the plain version in between.

The name tells you which you've got. A Greater Bonewrought Cuirass is the endgame version of the same piece.

Armour sets

Five sets of five pieces — helm, armour, gloves, boots, amulet. Wearing 2, 3, 4 or 5 pieces of one set stacks escalating set bonuses on top of the pieces themselves.

Mixed versions still count. A Lesser piece and a Greater piece both complete the set — but the bonus pays out at your weakest piece. So finish a Lesser set early, then lift it one slot at a time.

!armor bonewrought shows any set's pieces, bonuses and what you're missing.

Stat caps — you're never wasting a drop

Crit, dodge, lifesteal and attack speed all have ceilings so nobody becomes untouchable.

They're soft caps: everything up to about half the ceiling counts in full, and past that each extra point gives a little less. You always gain something from a second piece — you just can't reach the ceiling from one lucky drop.

Your stash

Every drop goes to your bag first, never straight onto your body. !stash to look, !equip 3 to wear, !sell 3 for gold.

The bag has a limit — when it fills, the oldest unlocked item is auto-sold. !lockstash 3 pins anything you don't want scrapped.

Enhancing

!upgrade weapon spends gold to add a ★ to the piece you're wearing, up to five. Each star raises that item's own stats by 20% and shrinks its drawbacks.

Stars stay with the item — if it's stolen or sold, they go with it. Selling refunds part of what you sank in. Set bonuses are never inflated by stars.

PvP & wagers

!challenge @someone for a straight duel — the winner may steal a piece of the loser's gear.

!duel @someone helm stakes a specific slot instead. They answer with !stake to put up their own, and both sides have to accept before anyone swings.

Relics

One-of-a-kind items. Only one exists in the whole arena and only one person can hold each. They can't be sold or given away — they change hands by defeat, or when the bearer runs !droprelic.

Go idle too long and your relic returns to the pool for someone else to win. !relics shows who holds what.

Raid boss

One enormous shared monster. !raidboss takes your turn against it — and its health does not reset between fighters. Everyone chips the same pool down together.

Dying costs you nothing; your turn just ends and your damage stands. Rewards scale with the share of the pool you removed, so a level 8 is paid on the same curve as a veteran. There's a short cooldown between turns, waived once the boss is nearly dead so anyone can pile in for the kill.

Ascension

At the level cap, !ascend resets you to level 1 and grants a permanent stat bump plus a roman-numeral badge (✦ III) that rides next to your level forever.

It also earns a naming token: !nameitem mints a brand-new item — named by you — into the drop pool for everybody, in all three power versions.

Each ascension taxes your XP gain a little, so every climb back up is longer than the last.
